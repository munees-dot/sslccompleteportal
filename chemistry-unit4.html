Gas Laws & Mole Concept

Chemistry · Class 10 · Unit 4 — Complete Study Tool

Chapter Summary

  • Gas molecules have very weak intermolecular attraction and are in constant, random motion in all directions (kinetic molecular theory).
  • Boyle's Law: at constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to pressure (V ∝ 1/P).
  • Charles's Law: at constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its Kelvin temperature (V ∝ T).
  • Absolute zero (0 K) = −273.15°C — the temperature at which a gas's volume would theoretically become zero.
  • Avogadro's Law: at constant temperature and pressure, the volume of a gas is directly proportional to the number of molecules it contains.
  • Ideal gas equation: PV = nRT (R = universal gas constant).
  • A mole is the amount of substance containing 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number).
  • At STP (273 K, 1 atm), 1 mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L — the molar volume.
  • The mole ratios in a balanced chemical equation let us calculate the amounts of reactants and products (stoichiometry).

Example: A gas occupies 150 L at 27°C — what is its volume at 0°C?

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ → 150/300 = V₂/273 → V₂ = 136.5 L

General Properties of Gases

Volume

Volume is the space occupied by a substance. A gas's volume is simply the volume of the container it occupies.

1000 cm³ = 1000 mL = 1 L  |  1 m³ = 1000 L  |  The SI unit of volume is m³.

Pressure

Gas pressure results from gas molecules colliding with the walls of their container. Pressure is the force experienced per unit area.

Pressure (P) = Force (F) / Area (A)  |  SI unit: Pascal (Pa)  |  1 atm = 1.01325 × 10⁵ Pa

Temperature

Heating a gas increases the kinetic energy of its molecules, and hence its temperature. The SI unit of temperature is Kelvin (K).

t°C = (t + 273) K

Postulates of the Kinetic Molecular Theory

  • Gases are made up of tiny particles (atoms/molecules).
  • The force of attraction between gas molecules is very weak.
  • Since the distance between molecules is very large, the actual volume of the molecules themselves is negligible compared to the total volume.
  • Gas molecules move constantly in all directions; this motion causes collisions between molecules and with the container walls.
  • Collisions between gas molecules are perfectly elastic — total kinetic energy before and after collision remains the same.
  • The average kinetic energy of gas molecules is directly proportional to its temperature.

The Gas Laws

a) Boyle's Law

Proposed by Robert Boyle in 1662, this law states: at constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure.

V ∝ 1/P (temperature, mass constant)  →  PV = k  →  P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Example: A fixed mass of gas has a volume of 44 L at 1 atm. What is its volume at 4 atm?

1 × 44 = 4 × V₂ → V₂ = 11 L

b) Charles's Law

Based on studies by Jacques Charles: at constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its temperature on the Kelvin scale.

V ∝ T (pressure, mass constant)  →  V/T = k  →  V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂
All V–T graphs (whatever the pressure) meet at −273.15°C — this is absolute zero (0 K), defined by Lord Kelvin.

Combined Gas Equation

Combining Boyle's Law and Charles's Law:

P₁V₁ / T₁ = P₂V₂ / T₂

Avogadro's Law

Equal volumes of any gas, at the same temperature and pressure, contain equal numbers of molecules.

V ∝ N (താപനില, മർദ്ദം സ്ഥിരം) / V ∝ N (temperature, pressure constant)

Ideal Gas Equation

Combining Boyle's Law, Charles's Law and Avogadro's Law:

PV = nRT  (R = Universal gas constant)

Gases that obey this equation at all temperatures and pressures are called ideal gases.

The Mole Concept

The mole is the unit used to count extremely tiny particles such as atoms, molecules and ions.

1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number, Nₐ)

This number is named the Avogadro number in honour of the Italian scientist Amedeo Avogadro.

Relative Atomic Mass

Relative atomic mass states how many times heavier an atom is than 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

Molar Mass

The sum of the masses of atoms in a molecule is its molecular mass. Expressed in grams, it becomes the molar mass — taking a compound in that amount always gives one mole of its molecules.

CompoundFormulaMolecular Mass
WaterH₂O18
Carbon dioxideCO₂44
AmmoniaNH₃17
GlucoseC₆H₁₂O₆180

Molar Volume of Gases at STP

At STP (273 K, 1 atm), 1 mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L
Number of moles (n) = given mass / molar mass  |  Number of molecules = n × 6.022 × 10²³  |  Volume at STP = n × 22.4 L

Example: How many moles in 44 g of CO₂ (molar mass 44)? = 44/44 = 1 mole. This 1 mole contains 6.022 × 10²³ molecules, and occupies 22.4 L at STP.

Mole Concept and Equations (Stoichiometry)

The coefficients in a balanced equation show the mole ratio between reactants and products.

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃

Here, 1 mole of nitrogen reacts with 3 moles of hydrogen to form 2 moles of ammonia.

On a Mass Basis

28 g N₂ + 6 g H₂ → 34 g NH₃

This ratio lets us find proportional amounts for reactants or products of any quantity.

Limiting Reagent

If the reactants aren't present in the exact mole ratio a reaction needs, the product formed is limited by whichever reactant runs out first.

Example: If 6 moles of nitrogen react with 6 moles of hydrogen (the required ratio is N:H = 1:3), only the hydrogen gets fully used — it reacts with just 2 moles of nitrogen, giving 4 moles of ammonia, leaving 4 moles of nitrogen unreacted.

A Combustion Example

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

Fully burning 16 g of methane (1 mole) requires 2 moles (64 g) of oxygen, producing 44 g of CO₂ and 36 g of water.
