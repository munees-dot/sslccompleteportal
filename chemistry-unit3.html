Periodic Table & Electron Configuration

Chemistry · Class 10 · Unit 3 — Complete Study Tool

Chapter Summary

  • The Bohr model has limitations, shown by de Broglie's dual (wave-particle) nature of matter and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle — leading to the quantum mechanical model of the atom.
  • Orbitals are regions around the nucleus where the probability of finding an electron is maximum.
  • Three quantum numbers describe orbitals and electrons: Principal (n), Azimuthal (l), and Magnetic (m).
  • The maximum number of electrons a shell can hold is 2n²; each orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons.
  • The Aufbau principle states that electrons fill sub-shells in order of increasing energy; the filling order can be found using the (n+l) value.
  • Chromium and copper show exceptions in electron configuration because half-filled (d⁵) or fully-filled (d¹⁰) d sub-shells are more stable.
  • An element's block (s, p, d, f), group number and period number can all be determined from its sub-shell electron configuration.
  • Ionisation enthalpy increases across a period (left to right) and decreases down a group.

Example: Electron Configuration of Magnesium

₁₂Mg → 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s²

The 12 electrons are spread across 3 shells (K, L, M) — since the highest shell number is 3 it belongs to Period 3, and since its outermost s sub-shell has 2 electrons it belongs to Group 2.

3. Magnetic Quantum Number (m)

Represents the orientation of the orbital in space. For a given l value, m can take (2l+1) values. E.g. l=0 (s) → 1 orientation; l=1 (p) → 3 orientations (3 p orbitals); l=2 (d) → 5 orientations (5 d orbitals).

Electron Configuration

The Aufbau Principle and the (n+l) Rule

Electrons fill sub-shells in order of increasing energy. A sub-shell with a higher (n+l) value has higher energy. If two sub-shells have the same (n+l) value, the one with the lower n has lower energy.

Example: 3d (n=3,l=2 → n+l=5) vs 4s (n=4,l=0 → n+l=4) 4s has the lower (n+l), so it has lower energy than 3d — hence 4s fills before 3d.

The energy of sub-shells increases in this order:

1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d ...

Shorthand Notation (Noble Gas Core)

Write the symbol of the nearest preceding noble gas in square brackets, followed by only the remaining sub-shell configuration. E.g. sodium's configuration (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹) can be written as [Ne] 3s¹.

The Special Cases of Cr and Cu

Based on the expected order, Cr (Z=24) should be [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s²; but its actual, stable configuration is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹. Similarly Cu (Z=29) is expected to be [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s², but its actual configuration is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.

Reason: electron arrangements d¹, d⁵ and d¹⁰ are more stable than others. So one electron shifts from 4s to 3d to achieve the more stable half-filled (d⁵) or fully-filled (d¹⁰) state.

Finding the Group Number

  • s-block: Group number = number of electrons in outermost s sub-shell (1 or 2).
  • p-block: Group number = total electrons in outermost s and p sub-shells + 10.
  • d-block: Group number = total electrons in the outermost s sub-shell and the inner d sub-shell.

Finding the Period Number

The highest principal quantum number (n) in an element's electron configuration is its period number.

Periodic Trends — Ionisation Enthalpy

Ionisation enthalpy is the minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from the outermost shell of an isolated gaseous atom.

Down a Group

  • Number of shells increases → outermost electron moves farther from the nucleus.
  • Nuclear attraction on the outer electron weakens → ionisation enthalpy decreases.
Elements with the lowest ionisation enthalpy: Caesium and Francium (bottom of the alkali metal group).

Across a Period (Left to Right)

  • Number of shells stays the same, but nuclear charge increases steadily.
  • Nuclear attraction on the outer electrons increases → ionisation enthalpy increases.
