Gas Laws & the Mole Concept — Revision Notes

1Kinetic Theory of Gases

  • Gases are made of tiny particles (atoms/molecules).
  • Attraction between gas molecules is very weak.
  • Distance between molecules is very large, so the volume occupied by the molecules themselves is negligible.
  • Molecules move constantly in all directions; collisions with the container wall cause gas pressure.
  • Collisions are elastic — total kinetic energy is conserved.
  • Average kinetic energy of molecules is directly proportional to temperature.

2Volume, Pressure & Temperature

Volume

The space needed for a substance to exist. A gas's volume is the volume of its container.

1000 cm³ = 1000 mL = 1 L1 m³ = 1000 L

Pressure

Force experienced per unit area due to gas molecules colliding with the container wall.

P = F / A

SI unit: Pascal (Pa = N/m²). Common unit: atmosphere (atm). 1 atm = 1.01325 × 10⁵ Pa. Measured using a manometer.

Temperature

SI unit: Kelvin (K). K = °C + 273

3Boyle's Law

At constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure.

V ∝ 1/PPV = kP₁V₁ = P₂V₂

The P–V graph is a hyperbola; PV is constant at every point.

Example: V = 44 L at 1 atm. Find V at 4 atm.
1×44 = 4×V₂ → V₂ = 11 L

Real life: weather balloons expand as they rise (pressure falls); bubbles rising from the sea floor grow bigger.

4Charles's Law

At constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its Kelvin temperature.

V ∝ TV/T = kV₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

Extending the V–T graph backwards, all lines meet at −273.15°C — theoretically zero volume. Lord Kelvin named this absolute zero and built the Kelvin scale on it.

CelsiusKelvin
−273°C0 K (absolute zero)
0°C273 K
100°C373 K
Example: V=150 L at 27°C (300K). Find V at 0°C (273K).
150/300 = V₂/273 → V₂ = 136.5 L

5Avogadro's Law & Combined Gas Equation

At constant temperature and pressure, the volume of a gas is directly proportional to the number of its molecules.

V ∝ N

Combining Boyle's Law (V∝1/P) and Charles's Law (V∝T):

P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂
Example: V=30L at 1atm,300K. Find V at 0.5atm,273K.
(1×30)/300 = (0.5×V₂)/273 → V₂ = 54.6 L

6The Mole Concept & Avogadro Number

A mole is the amount of substance containing 6.022 × 10²³ particles (atoms/molecules/ions) — the Avogadro number (Nₐ), named after Amedeo Avogadro.

8Molar Volume at STP & Ideal Gas Equation

STP: 273 K, 1 atm

At STP, 1 mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L — the molar volume at STP.

moles = volume (L) / 22.4

Combining Boyle's, Charles's and Avogadro's Laws:

PV = nRT

R = universal gas constant. Gases that obey this at all T and P are called ideal gases.

9Mole Concept in Chemical Equations

Coefficients in a balanced equation give the mole ratio of reactants and products.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O  ·  4 g H₂ + 32 g O₂ → 36 g H₂O

Example: 40 g H₂ reacts fully with O₂. Water formed?
4g→36g, so 1g→9g, so 40g→9×40 = 360 g H₂O

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ — mole ratio N:H:NH₃ = 1:3:2. A shortage of any reactant limits the product proportionally.
